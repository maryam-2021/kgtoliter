# Diesel kg to Litre Conversion Chart

In commercial fleet operations, fuel bunkering, haulage logistics, and agriculture, fuel is often **purchased and weighed in kilograms or metric tonnes (t)**, but **dispensed and consumed in fluid litres (L)**.

The standard conversion baseline for commercial automotive diesel under European standard **EN 590** and **ASTM D975** is:

- **Standard Diesel Density at 15°C:** `0.832 kg/L` (832 kg/m³)
- **1 Kilogram of Diesel = 1.202 Litres** (1,201.92 mL)
- **1 Litre of Diesel = 0.832 Kilograms** (832 grams)
- **1 Metric Tonne (1,000 kg) of Diesel = 1,201.92 Litres**

## 3. How to Calculate Diesel Mass and Volume

### The Standard Formulas:

`Volume in Litres (L) = Mass (kg) ÷ Density (kg/L)`

`Mass in Kilograms (kg) = Volume (L) × Density (kg/L)`

### Practical Example: Bunkering 12.5 Metric Tonnes of Diesel
1. Convert tonnes to kg: `12.5 t × 1,000 = 12,500 kg`
2. Apply density formula: `12,500 kg ÷ 0.832 kg/L = 15,024.04 Litres`
3. *Result: A 12.5-tonne delivery requires 15,024 litres of storage tank capacity.*

## 5. Effect of Temperature on Fuel Volume (ASTM D1298)

Liquid hydrocarbons expand with heat. A mass of diesel that occupies 1,000 litres at a winter temperature of 0°C will expand to over 1,025 litres at 35°C, even though the physical mass remains identical:

- **Thermal Expansion Coefficient for Diesel (&beta;):** `~0.00083 per °C`
- **Formula:** `Volume(T) = Volume(15°C) × [1 + 0.00083 × (T - 15°C)]`

For commercial custody transfers, automatic temperature compensation (ATC) meters standardize billing to the 15°C reference volume.
