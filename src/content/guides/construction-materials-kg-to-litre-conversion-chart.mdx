# Construction Materials kg to Litre Conversion Chart

In civil engineering, general contracting, and masonry, building supplies are **quoted and delivered by the tonne or kilogram (mass)**, but excavations, formwork pours, and trenches are **measured in cubic metres (m³) and fluid litres (L)**.

Note that in metric measurement:
- **1 Cubic Metre (m³) = 1,000 Litres (L)**
- **1 Litre (L) = 0.001 Cubic Metres (m³)**

## 3. Contractor Site Formulas: Estimating Tonnages from Dimensions

### Step 1: Calculate Void Volume in Cubic Metres
`Volume (m³) = Length (m) × Width (m) × Depth (m)`

### Step 2: Convert Volume to Litres
`Volume in Litres = Volume (m³) × 1,000`

### Step 3: Calculate Required Mass in Metric Tonnes
`Mass in Tonnes (t) = Volume (m³) × Bulk Density (t/m³ or kg/L)`

---

### Practical Contractor Example: Ordering Gravel for a Trench
- **Trench Dimensions:** 15 metres long, 0.8 metres wide, 0.2 metres deep.
- **Gravel Bulk Density:** `1.55 t/m³` (1.55 kg/L).
- **Calculation:**
  1. Volume = `15m × 0.8m × 0.2m = 2.4 m³` (2,400 Litres).
  2. Mass = `2.4 m³ × 1.55 t/m³ = 3.72 Metric Tonnes` (3,720 kg).
  3. Adding a 10% compaction & wastage buffer: `3.72 t × 1.10 = 4.09 Tonnes`.
- *Result: Order 4.1 tonnes (or approx. 5 bulk bags of 850kg) of crushed stone.*

## 4. Why Bulk Density Varies on Construction Sites

1. **Moisture Bulking**: Dry sand bulks in volume by up to 25% when slightly damp (5% moisture) due to surface tension holding grains apart. When fully saturated, it settles tightly again.
2. **Mechanical Compaction**: Loose aggregates settle by 15% to 30% after roller or plate compactor passes, increasing density per m³.
3. **Aggregate Grading**: Blended well-graded aggregates (where small particles fill the voids between large stones) are significantly denser than single-size gravels.
