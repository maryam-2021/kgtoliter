# Comprehensive Liquid Density Chart (2026 Reference)

In engineering design, commercial custody transfer, laboratory chemical synthesis, and culinary recipe formulation, having access to certified density values is essential for accurate conversions between kilograms (mass) and litres (volume).

## 4. Temperature Correction and Mathematical Rules

Liquid density decreases predictably as fluid temperature increases due to thermal agitation:

`Density(T) = Initial Density ÷ [1 + β × (T - T_0)]`

Where:
- `β` is the volumetric thermal expansion coefficient of the fluid.
- `T - T_0` is the temperature change in degrees Celsius.

For example, when pumping 20,000 litres of diesel in winter at 0°C compared to summer at 30°C, the temperature difference of 30°C expands the liquid volume by approximately 2.5%, equivalent to 500 extra litres of space for the same mass of fuel!

## 5. How to Convert Between Mass and Volume

Using the values in this chart, conversions are calculated via two core formulas:

1. **Litres to Kilograms (Volume to Mass)**:
   `Mass (kg) = Volume (L) × Density (kg/L)`
   *Example: 50 Litres of Acetone = 50 × 0.791 = 39.55 kg*

2. **Kilograms to Litres (Mass to Volume)**:
   `Volume (L) = Mass (kg) ÷ Density (kg/L)`
   *Example: 50 kg of Glycerin = 50 ÷ 1.261 = 39.65 Litres*
