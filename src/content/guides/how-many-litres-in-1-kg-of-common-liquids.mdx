# How Many Litres Are in 1 kg of Common Liquids?

The quick answer is: **1 kg of liquid does not always equal 1 litre**. Because kilograms measure physical mass and litres measure volume, the exact volume occupied by 1 kg depends directly on the liquid's **density** (&rho;).

- If a liquid is **denser than water** (density above 1.00 kg/L), 1 kg occupies **less than 1 litre** (for example, honey occupies 0.704 L).
- If a liquid is **lighter than water** (density below 1.00 kg/L), 1 kg occupies **more than 1 litre** (for example, petrol occupies 1.342 L).

## 2. In-Depth Liquid Breakdown & Scientific Mechanics

### 1. Water (1 kg = 1.000 L)
Pure water serves as the universal baseline for the metric system. At 4°C (peak density), exactly 1 kg of pure water occupies 1.000 L (1,000 mL). At room temperature (20°C), slight thermal expansion reduces density to 0.998 kg/L, meaning 1 kg = 1.002 L.

### 2. Milk (1 kg = 0.971 L)
Commercial cows' milk is denser than water because it contains dissolved milk solids, mineral salts, casein proteins, and lactose sugars.
- **Whole Milk** (1.030 kg/L): 1 kg = 0.971 L (971 mL)
- **Semi-Skimmed** (1.033 kg/L): 1 kg = 0.968 L (968 mL)
- **Skimmed Milk** (1.035 kg/L): 1 kg = 0.966 L (966 mL)

### 3. Cooking Oil (1 kg = 1.087 L)
Plant-derived culinary oils (vegetable oil, olive oil, sunflower oil, canola oil) consist of triglyceride lipids that are less dense than water.
- **Vegetable Oil** (0.920 kg/L): 1 kg = 1.087 L
- **Extra Virgin Olive Oil** (0.918 kg/L): 1 kg = 1.089 L
- **Canola Oil** (0.915 kg/L): 1 kg = 1.093 L

Because oil density is below 1.00 kg/L, cooking oil floats on water and occupies roughly 8.7% more volume than water for the same weight.

### 4. Diesel Fuel (1 kg = 1.202 L)
Under European standard **EN 590** and ASTM D975, automotive diesel has a standard custody reference density of 0.832 kg/L at 15°C. Converting 1 kg of diesel yields 1.202 litres (1,202 mL). Consequently, a 20 kg jerrycan of diesel holds 24.04 litres.

### 5. Petrol / Gasoline (1 kg = 1.342 L)
Automotive petrol is composed of light aliphatic and aromatic hydrocarbons with an average density of 0.745 kg/L at 15°C. Because petrol is significantly lighter than water, 1 kg yields 1.342 litres (1,342 mL). A 50 L car fuel tank weighs only approximately 37.25 kg of petrol payload.

### 6. Natural Honey (1 kg = 0.704 L)
Pure honey is a supersaturated aqueous solution of fructose (38%) and glucose (31%) with low moisture content (under 18%). This tight molecular packing gives honey a high density of 1.420 kg/L. Therefore, 1 kg of honey occupies only 0.704 litres (704 mL). A standard 500 mL jar holds approximately 710 grams of honey.

### 7. Paint (1 kg = 0.741 L to 0.833 L)
Paint consists of solvent binders, titanium dioxide pigments, and mineral fillers (calcium carbonate).
- **Water-Based Acrylic / Latex Wall Paint** (~1.350 kg/L): 1 kg = 0.741 L (741 mL). A standard 5 L tub weighs ~6.75 kg.
- **Oil-Based Gloss Enamel** (~1.200 kg/L): 1 kg = 0.833 L (833 mL).

### 8. Alcohol (1 kg = 1.267 L)
- **Pure Ethanol (100%)**: Density 0.789 kg/L &rarr; 1 kg = 1.267 L (1,267 mL).
- **Rubbing Alcohol (Isopropyl 70%)**: Density 0.878 kg/L &rarr; 1 kg = 1.139 L (1,139 mL).
- **Vodka / Spirits (40% ABV)**: Density 0.948 kg/L &rarr; 1 kg = 1.055 L (1,055 mL).

### 9. Motor Engine Oil (1 kg = 1.143 L)
Standard synthetic and mineral automotive engine lubricants (such as SAE 0W-20, 5W-30, and 10W-40) have an average room temperature density of 0.875 kg/L.
- 1 kg of motor oil = 1.143 litres (1,143 mL).
- A typical 5 L oil jug weighs approximately 4.375 kg.

## 3. The Core Conversion Formula

To calculate litres for any quantity of liquid, use the standard mass-to-volume formula:

`Volume (L) = Mass (kg) ÷ Density (kg/L)`

### Example: How many litres are in 25 kg of Cooking Oil?
1. Given: `Mass = 25 kg`, `Density = 0.920 kg/L`.
2. Apply formula: `Volume = 25 ÷ 0.920 = 27.174 L`.
3. Result: 25 kg of cooking oil equals **27.174 litres**.

## 5. Frequently Asked Questions

### Which liquid has the highest litres per kg?
Among common everyday liquids, **petrol (gasoline)** has the highest volume per kilogram (~1.342 L/kg) due to its low density (0.745 kg/L).

### Which common liquid has the lowest litres per kg?
**Honey** has one of the lowest volumes per kilogram (~0.704 L/kg) because its high dissolved sugar content makes it exceptionally dense (1.420 kg/L).

### Does temperature change the number of litres in 1 kg?
Yes. When liquids are heated, thermal expansion causes molecules to spread out, lowering density and **increasing the litres occupied by 1 kg**. For example, warm frying oil at 180°C expands to ~1.136 L per kg compared to 1.087 L per kg at room temperature.
